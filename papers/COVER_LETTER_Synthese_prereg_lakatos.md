Dear Professor Bueno and Synthese Editorial Board,

I am pleased to submit the enclosed manuscript, **"Pre-Registered Lakatos Rigorous Test for Algebra-Based Physics Programmes — A Methodology Paper"**, for consideration as a research article in *Synthese*.

The paper proposes a methodological protocol — the **Pre-Registered Lakatos Rigorous Test** — for discriminating genuine novel predictions from numerology in algebra-based physics programmes (hypercomplex algebras, Clifford algebras, exceptional Lie groups). These programmes face a structural epistemic hazard: their predictions are derived from finite algebraic primitives yielding small integers and simple ratios that *trivially* match Standard Model observables under the look-elsewhere effect. Without a rigorous discrimination protocol, retrospective matches are nearly inevitable, and Lakatos's progressive-vs-degenerating dichotomy cannot be applied honestly.

The proposed protocol consists of eight pre-registered steps culminating in a cryptographic sha256 commit of the prediction list *before* observable comparison — adapting the LHC blind-analysis precedent (ATLAS/CMS Higgs 2012) from experimental to theoretical prediction sets. A Monte Carlo null model and Bonferroni look-elsewhere correction follow. The protocol is applied as a case study to the ICE_ORCA_DRAGON sedenion programme, yielding 0/15 SIGNAL_GENUINE under the protocol's threshold — the first cryptographically-committed Lakatos-rigorous test of a hypercomplex programme on record.

I believe the paper is suitable for *Synthese* because:
1. It contributes a novel methodological tool to philosophy of physics, generalizing across a class of programmes (hypercomplex, Clifford, exceptional Lie — Furey, Gillard-Gresnigt, Trayling-Baylis, Lisi, Catto, Connes-Marcolli);
2. It bridges philosophy of science (Lakatos lineage, Tüchsen 2024 Stagnant class, Mayo severe testing) with statistical methodology (Bonferroni, Holm, BH-FDR, MC null modeling);
3. It places the foundational discipline of pre-registration (Nosek et al. 2018, Chambers 2013) in dialogue with the algebra-based physics community for the first time.

The manuscript is approximately 5,800 words (excluding references and appendices), with 27 references and 2 appendices (28-primitive list of ICE; 20 PDG frozen observable set). All Python code, sha256 commit log, and raw results JSONs are openly available; the protocol is *fully reproducible*.

The companion paper "*Asymmetric Lakatos Verdict via Fiber-Stratified Functor Evaluation*" (under simultaneous consideration at EJPS) establishes the theoretical fiber-stratification underlying why the present protocol yields meaningful negative results without invalidating the algebra-axis Progressive verdict of the same programme. Reviewers may wish to consider both papers together; they are designed to be readable independently.

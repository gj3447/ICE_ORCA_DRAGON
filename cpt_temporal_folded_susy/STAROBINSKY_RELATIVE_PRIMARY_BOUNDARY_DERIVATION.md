# Starobinsky relative primary boundary reduction

2026-09-08 · `SUPPORTING_METHOD` · analytic derivation.

## One question, one scoped output, and one non-claim

**Question.** For the declared homogeneous Starobinsky boundary BFV system,
do either of two complementary primary slices give a Q-invariant coisotropic
reduction to the same matter/minimal BFV boundary system while making the
actual displayed source endpoint terms vanish under their own allowed boundary
variations?

**Output.** The momentum/antifield slice
\(M=\{\Pi=\bar c=0\}\) and coordinate/ghost slice
\(P(N_0)=\{N=N_0,\rho=0\}\) are Q-invariant coisotropics. Their primary
characteristic quotients both give the same reduced matter/minimal BFV system.
The source boundary terms \(+[\bar c\rho]_{\partial I}\) and
\(-[\bar c\,\delta N]_{\partial I}\) vanish separately on the corresponding
allowed boundary data.

**Non-claim.** This is not a classification of all BFV polarizations, a full
bulk BV gauge-fixing Lagrangian, a quantum pushforward/kernel, a physical state
space or product, a determinant/half-density construction, an original complex
integration cycle, or a Gate-1 result.

The dominant false inference controlled here is to equate a primary-sector
coisotropic/Lagrangian reduction with a full boundary polarization or with
quantum CPT sewing.

## 1. Fixed boundary convention

target from

§§1–3, especially equations (1)–(4), (7)–(13). The boundary coordinates are

\[
(a,\phi,N;p_a,p_\phi,\Pi\mid c,\bar\rho,\rho,\bar c),
\]

with even degree-zero symplectic primitive and BFV charge

\[
 \alpha_{\mathrm{BFV}}
 =p_a\,da+p_\phi\,d\phi+\Pi\,dN+\bar\rho\,dc+\bar c\,d\rho,
 \qquad \omega_{\mathrm{BFV}}=d\alpha_{\mathrm{BFV}},
 \tag{1}
\]

\[
 \Omega=cH_L+\rho\Pi,
 \qquad
 H_L=-\frac{p_a^2}{24\pi^2a}+\frac{p_\phi^2}{4\pi^2a^3}
 -6\pi^2a+\frac32\pi^2a^3(1-e^{-\sqrt{2/3}\phi})^2,\quad a>0.
 \tag{2}
\]

The declared even and odd bracket convention gives

\[
 QN=-\rho,\qquad Q\bar c=\Pi,\qquad Q\Pi=Q\rho=0,
 \tag{3}
\]

alongside the reduced matter/minimal action generated by \(cH_L\). Since
\(H_L\) is independent of \(N\) and \(\{H_L,\Pi\}=0\), \(\{\Omega,\Omega\}=0\).

## 2. Source endpoint data and signs

The primary local jet rewriting uses

\[
 \sigma^+=-\bar c.
 \tag{4}
\]

The two relative endpoint terms retained by the source derivation are

\[
 -[\sigma^+\rho]_{\partial I}=+[\bar c\rho]_{\partial I},
 \qquad
 +[\sigma^+\delta N]_{\partial I}=-[\bar c\,\delta N]_{\partial I}.
 \tag{5}
\]

In particular, (3) implies \(Q\sigma^+=-\Pi\). This sign is tied to the
source convention. Write \([f]_{\partial I}=f(s_+)-f(s_-)\), as in the local
source derivation. Below each integrand vanishes at each endpoint separately.

## 3. Momentum/antifield slice \(M\)

Define

\[
 M=\{\Pi=0,\ \bar c=0\},\qquad I_M=\langle\Pi,\bar c\rangle.
 \tag{6}
\]

Equation (3) gives \(QI_M\subset I_M\), so \(M\) is Q-invariant. Its two
constraints mutually Poisson commute. It is therefore coisotropic. The
primary restriction of (1) has characteristic kernel

\[
 \ker(\iota_M^*\omega_{\rm BFV})_{\rm primary}
   =\operatorname{span}\{\partial_N,\partial_\rho\},
 \tag{7}
\]

so \((N,\rho)\) are its characteristic fibres. Restricted primitive and charge
are basic for that kernel and equal the pullback of the reduced system:

\[
 \iota_M^*\alpha_{\rm BFV}=p_a da+p_\phi d\phi+\bar\rho dc,
 \qquad
 \iota_M^*\Omega=cH_L.
 \tag{8}
\]

The source terms in (5) vanish because \(\bar c=0\), with no need to fix
\(N\), \(\rho\), or their allowed variations.

## 4. Coordinate/ghost slice \(P(N_0)\)

For a declared real \(N_0\) in the lapse chart (take \(N_0>0\) when using
the positive-lapse body chart), define

\[
 P(N_0)=\{N=N_0,\ \rho=0\},\qquad
 I_P=\langle N-N_0,\rho\rangle.
 \tag{9}
\]

Now \(Q(N-N_0)=-\rho\) and \(Q\rho=0\), hence \(QI_P\subset I_P\).
The defining constraints again mutually Poisson commute, so this is a second
Q-invariant coisotropic. Its primary characteristic kernel is

\[
 \ker(\iota_P^*\omega_{\rm BFV})_{\rm primary}
   =\operatorname{span}\{\partial_\Pi,\partial_{\bar c}\}.
 \tag{10}
\]

Thus \((\Pi,\bar c)\) are the characteristic fibres and

\[
 \iota_P^*\alpha_{\rm BFV}=p_a da+p_\phi d\phi+\bar\rho dc,
 \qquad
 \iota_P^*\Omega=cH_L.
 \tag{11}
\]

Here \(\rho=0\) kills the first term in (5). The boundary condition
\(N=N_0\) means allowed variations obey \(\delta N=0\), which kills the
second term even though \(\bar c\) remains free.
The allowed tangent variations also obey \(\delta\rho=0\), as needed in
(11). No physical principle selects \(N_0\) here, and no global finite-lapse
translation or compact test-space invariance follows from this local slice.

## 5. Dimension, Lagrangian qualification, and relative correspondence

The full boundary space has superdimension \((6\mid4)\). Both \(M\) and
\(P(N_0)\) have \((5\mid3)\); their characteristic distributions have
\((1\mid1)\), so their reductions have \((4\mid2)\). That quotient is the
same matter/minimal BFV boundary space

\[
 \mathcal B_{\rm red}=(a,\phi;p_a,p_\phi\mid c,\bar\rho),
 \quad \alpha_{\rm red}=p_a da+p_\phi d\phi+\bar\rho dc,
 \quad \Omega_{\rm red}=cH_L.
 \tag{12}
\]

The primary sector itself has superdimension \((2\mid2)\), with coordinates
\((N,\Pi\mid\rho,\bar c)\). The primary pieces of both slices have
\((1\mid1)\) and zero restricted primary two-form. They are therefore graded
Lagrangian **in the primary sector**. They are not Lagrangian subspaces of the
full boundary BFV space, because the matter/minimal symplectic sector remains
nondegenerate. In the full space each is coisotropic.

Consequently the correct classical relation is the pair of reduction maps

\[
 M\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal B_{\rm red}
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 P(N_0)\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal B_{\rm red},
 \tag{13}
\]

or, if an actual relation is needed, the fibre product
\(M\times_{\mathcal B_{\rm red}}P(N_0)\). There is no canonical
unreduced map \(M\to P(N_0)\) supplied by this calculation. The equality of
(8) and (11) is exactly the required classical relative BFV correspondence.

More precisely let \(r_M,r_P\) forget the primary coordinates and let
\(\iota_M,\iota_P\) be the slice inclusions. For either \(X=M,P\),
\[
 \iota_X^*\alpha_{\rm BFV}=r_X^*\alpha_{\rm red},\qquad
 \iota_X^*\Omega=r_X^*\Omega_{\rm red},\qquad
 dr_X Q=Q_{\rm red}\circ r_X.
 \tag{14}
\]
The last equality holds on the six reduced coordinate generators because
the primary charge has zero bracket with each of them. The derivation rule
extends it to smooth coefficient superfunctions in the declared chart.
Explicitly \(Q_{\rm red}a=cp_a/(12\pi^2a)\),
\(Q_{\rm red}\phi=-cp_\phi/(2\pi^2a^3)\),
\(Q_{\rm red}p_a=c\partial_aH_L\),
\(Q_{\rm red}p_\phi=c\partial_\phi H_L\),
\(Q_{\rm red}c=0\), and \(Q_{\rm red}\bar\rho=H_L\).

The graph \(\mathcal R_X=\{(\iota_X(x),r_X(x)):x\in X\}\) is a
graded Lagrangian canonical relation in
\(\mathcal B_{\rm ext}^{-}\times\mathcal B_{\rm red}\): (14) makes the
difference primitive vanish, and its dimension \((5\mid3)\) is half the
product dimension \((10\mid6)\). This relation is not an isomorphism of
unreduced slices and does not identify quantum complexes.

## 6. Controls against boundary cancellation alone

There are three related control groups: actual graded bracket and projected
Q identities; relative one-form/charge pullbacks and characteristic blocks;
and isolated-condition falsifiers. The runner retains every exact expression.

* \(\bar c=0\) alone kills both displayed source residues but is not
  Q-invariant: \(Q\bar c=\Pi\) can be nonzero. The partner \(\Pi=0\)
  repairs this precise failure.
* \(\rho=0\) alone kills the action residue but leaves
  \(-\bar c\delta N\). A nonzero formal odd generator \(\bar c\) and
  \(\delta N=1\) witness the failure; odd variables are never assigned an
  ordinary nonzero real value.
* \(N=N_0\) alone fails tangency through \(QN=-\rho\). Its partner
  \(\rho=0\) repairs the failure and \(\delta N=0\) kills the primitive.

No matter constraint has been imposed: at \(a=1,\phi=p_a=p_\phi=0\),
\(H_L=-6\pi^2\ne0\) in both reduced targets. The nondegenerate remaining
matter/minimal two-form also rules out calling the full slices Lagrangian.
Other primary slices may exist; these checks do not classify them.

The Lean companion proves only the finite complex-linear generator model
\((n,\Pi,\rho,\bar c)\), \(n=N-N_0\), its square-zero map,
two invariant subspaces, commuting complementary projections, and scalar
one-form coefficients. The actual odd algebra is checked separately by the
graded symbolic calculation. Neither method formalizes a BV integration or
analytic physical state space.
